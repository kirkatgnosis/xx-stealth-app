# Seven compound workflows to investigate

Prepared September 12, 2026. These are hypotheses, not selected products or an MVP specification. Customer language below is invented for testing, not a quotation from interviews. US-first and English-first are working assumptions. A native app is the intended eventual business; a service pilot can test the job before proving demand for the app.

The screen favors a meaningful outcome, a real dependency between concerns, an identifiable buying occasion, and observable substitutes. It does not rank ease of engineering. C1–C3 received independent current-source research. C4–C7 received a conceptual screen informed by the earlier research; their low rank reflects weaker evidence, not evidence of absence.

## C1. Have the capacity for the life I want this week

**Customer and trigger.** A woman roughly 40–60 who previously exercised regularly or bought strength programming, and whose recent body/life transition has made her old training, meals and sleep routine incompatible with her actual week. She has a specific activity she wants to keep doing: energetic workdays, gardening, dancing, or outings. Neither weight loss nor menopause medication use is required.

**Likely language.** “I want enough strength and energy to do the things I enjoy, without organizing my whole life around getting healthy.”

**Current combination to investigate.** A fitness subscription or trainer, wearable/phone sleep information, recipes or meal service, personal calendar, and sometimes clinician advice. Co-purchase and frustrating interactions in the same person are still unmeasured.

**Connection and workflow.** Begin with one desired real-life activity and the coming week's constraints. Translate them into a reviewed minimum movement plan, two feasible meal/preparation decisions, and a workable rest schedule. Produce a usable first session and tonight's practical plan immediately. After a disrupted night or work change, revise the *whole feasible week*: move a session, choose an appropriate reviewed alternative, and change meal preparation effort. Return to the desired activity and judge whether it became easier to participate. Health questions outside the plan go to an appropriate professional.

**Why more than strength alone?** A good session plan can still fail if it ignores the time, food preparation and rest decisions needed to carry it out. The proposed value is reducing conflicts between those decisions. This must improve actual participation beyond an equally good strength program. Merely displaying workouts, recipes and sleep content together fails the thesis.

**Payment, native and AI.** Proposed payment buys an achievable program and adaptive coordination. Existing fitness payments are an anchor, not proof of this premium. A phone can deliver sessions offline, capture changing constraints quickly, and support kitchen/gym use. AI can translate messy constraints into choices within expert-approved alternatives and explain tradeoffs; it cannot infer a safe workout or hormonal cause from poor sleep alone.

**Expertise and strongest failure.** Qualified exercise programming, dietetic review where appropriate, credible menopause education, and escalation boundaries are material costs. Respin and WW/Pvolve already integrate much of this; Noom supplies a broad free GLP-1 substitute. The customer may primarily need symptom treatment or a trusted human coach. A broad independent subscription may add decisions instead of reducing them. [Independent disconfirmation](tracks/Midlife-Capacity-Disconfirmation.md).

## C2. Be ready for my first weeks back in visible work

**Customer and trigger.** A woman returning to client-facing or hybrid work in four to eight weeks after at least six months primarily at home, on caregiving leave or working remotely. Her current clothes, comfort needs and daily logistics no longer fit the actual role. Recruit care-return and remote-return cohorts separately; they are not one measured market. Do not presume a preferred style, size, femininity or need to appear younger.

**Likely language.** “I want to walk into my first weeks feeling prepared and like myself, without buying a whole new wardrobe.”

**Current combination to investigate.** Existing clothes, saved outfits, shopping/returns, a tailor or stylist, commute/weather information, work calendar, and advice from peers. For a parent who wants it, clothing may need to accommodate pumping; this is a stated constraint, not a universal requirement.

**Connection and workflow.** Start with three actual upcoming work situations and 12–20 relevant items that currently fit comfortably. Assemble one complete, usable first-day outfit immediately. Reconcile footwear, outerwear, bag, dress context and commute. Rehearse the outfit sitting, walking and doing the customer's actual day. Identify whether one alteration or purchase unlocks several outfits; maintain a user-chosen spending cap. Use real wear feedback during the first two weeks to adjust the next combinations, care/laundry rotation and return decisions. The result is clothes that work in real situations, not an attractive digital collage.

**Why more than styling alone?** Attractive outfits can fail on commute comfort, pumping access, weather or turnaround between office days. Linking a wear rehearsal to the actual calendar could prevent unusable purchases and morning rework. If a competent stylist already captures all these constraints in one consultation, there is no distinct bundle advantage.

**Payment, native and AI.** Test a four-week outcome purchase, with an optional later seasonal/occasion refresh. Customers may pay for buying fewer wrong items and having a usable return wardrobe. Camera capture in the closet/fitting room, outfit reference at departure, receipt sharing and rapid feedback support native use. AI drafts combinations from the customer's own items and constraints and groups a constrained gap list. It must not claim to measure fit accurately from photos or invent purchasable inventory. Human taste and fitting judgment remain important.

**Expertise and strongest failure.** A stylist should review practical coherence; a tailor assesses alterations. No medical or body-evaluation service is implied. Indyx already combines a closet, outfit/calendar use and paid human styling. Free tools and store stylists may solve the job. The “whole day” additions may be a better stylist intake questionnaire rather than a separate app business. [Independent diligence](tracks/Work-Return-Diligence.md).

## C3. Enjoy the active trip I have already booked

**Customer and trigger.** A woman roughly 45–65 with a guided walking/hiking holiday booked six to twelve weeks away, worried about enjoying the advertised days without letting down companions. Exclude technical mountaineering and requests for rehabilitation or medical clearance. Age is an initial recruitment choice, not a definition of capacity.

**Likely language.** “I have paid for this trip. I want to enjoy the walking days and know my shoes and pack will work before I get there.”

**Current combination to investigate.** Operator itinerary and packing PDF, ordinary workouts or trainer, outdoor store advice, gear purchases/returns, trial walks, weather and packing lists. A customer with an expensive holiday has demonstrated travel spend, not preparation-app willingness to pay.

**Connection and workflow.** Confirm actual itinerary demands with the operator. Pair a qualified progression with the customer's available preparation time. Immediately deliver a first-week action and a practical shoe/pack trial. Connect preparation walks to testing the equipment and layers she intends to bring. Revise load, gear or the plan after actual experience, within professional scope. Produce a pack assembled from tested items and questions for the operator. During travel, provide offline access to her agreed plan and packing information; after travel, debrief what worked before any offer for the next trip.

**Why more than training alone?** Fitness preparation and gear selection are dependent: an unused shoe can fail on a long descent, and a planned load changes training demands. Training and packing should change each other. Generic advice in two tabs is insufficient. The decisive result is identifying and resolving a real preparation/gear incompatibility that the training-only or operator baseline misses.

**Payment, native and AI.** A limited eight-week preparation purchase fits the occasion better than assumed perpetual subscription. A native app helps on trial walks and offline travel, using photos, route/activity capture by consent and ready access to checked plans. AI can extract itinerary constraints into a reviewable brief and connect user-confirmed gear experience with approved program options. It cannot certify readiness, forecast safety or substitute for current operator/local guidance.

**Expertise and strongest failure.** Qualified training, current operator demands, trustworthy gear information and referral for pain/medical questions are needed. HikeStrong already supplies trip-specific adaptive training; AdventureWomen's preparation partner combines coaching and packing. Operator free advice may be enough. Natural graduation and infrequent travel could make this a modest service business rather than a retained app subscription. [Independent diligence](tracks/Active-Trip-Diligence.md).

## C4. Take my planned break while care at home still works

**Customer and trigger.** A employed woman who is a parent's primary family-care organizer, has a real work trip or personal break in the next month, and has at least one willing alternate helper. The household, not every helper, is the purchasing unit.

**Likely language.** “I need to be away for three days without running everything from my phone.”

**Current combination to investigate.** Family messages, provider portals, medication lists, service bookings, calls, paid care and personal memory. The earlier caregiving research supports coordination burden and financial strain; it does not establish demand for this full outcome.

**Connection and workflow.** Reconstruct one real day of care and each helper's availability. Immediately identify the first uncovered task and draft one recipient-ready shift brief. With human confirmation, turn gaps into accepted helper assignments or options to book paid help. Rehearse the handoff before departure, confirm where authoritative instructions and escalation contacts reside, and define who handles exceptions. After return, reconcile changes and reuse the arrangement for another absence.

**Incremental value.** A notebook records care; a workable temporary coverage arrangement removes the organizer from the critical path. Information, actual availability and acceptance belong together because a correct checklist without a responsible helper does not deliver a break.

**Payment/native/AI.** An episodic coverage-preparation fee could be justified if it makes a real absence possible. Voice/camera capture, accessible briefs and offline access help at home. AI organizes gaps and drafts instructions from exact approved sources. Availability and acceptance are human facts, never inferred from an unanswered message.

**Expertise and strongest failure.** Medical instructions remain clinician-authored; professional care assessment, hiring, safeguarding and emergency response require real people/services. The binding problem may be missing affordable labor. Free coordination apps already exist, and consumers under financial strain may not pay for organizing. This concept earns a new investigation only with evidence of coverage resources and buyer budget. It must not promise to procure or deliver care without that capability.

## C5. Make my first home on my own work within my budget

**Customer and trigger.** A woman moving into her first solo household after a separation or long period living with others, with keys or a lease date in the next six weeks. Recruit only after her housing decision; do not assume vulnerability creates spending power.

**Likely language.** “I want a home that works from the first week, and I need to know what to buy, book and learn in what order.”

**Current combination to investigate.** Lease/building instructions, moving checklists, marketplace furniture, retailer delivery windows, utility websites, friends and tradespeople.

**Connection and workflow.** Start with actual rooms, move date, items already owned and spending ceiling. Deliver a first-night setup with a dependency-resolved shopping/booking sequence. Check dimensions and delivery/access constraints before buying. Allocate the budget across essentials and deferred purchases. Photograph installed equipment and assemble manufacturer-backed operating instructions. Revisit after living there to resolve remaining setup and maintenance tasks.

**Incremental value.** Layout, budget, purchase order and delivery dependencies affect each other. A room design without delivery/access or essential utilities is not a livable first week. The value could be fewer wrong purchases and failed bookings.

**Payment/native/AI.** A move/setup package is more plausible than an annual subscription. On-site camera capture, verified measurements and checklists help; AI can organize manuals, options and dependencies, with dimensions and prices confirmed by the customer. A general AI conversation plus checklists is a strong free baseline.

**Expertise and strongest failure.** Tradespeople handle hazardous work; no legal, financial or housing decisions are delegated to AI. Actual assembly and installation may drive nearly all value. This is an episodic, broad service task with no women-specific paid advantage established. Desk evidence is too thin to advance ahead of C1–C3.

## C6. Restart independent professional work around the time I really have

**Customer and trigger.** A woman with prior professional expertise returning after caregiving, choosing a small consulting/freelance engagement rather than a full-time job, with a specific former contact or genuine opportunity to pursue in the next month.

**Likely language.** “I want to win a first manageable project and deliver it well, without promising hours I do not have.”

**Current combination to investigate.** Old portfolio/CV, former colleagues, general AI writing, calendar, proposal templates, childcare arrangements and spreadsheets.

**Connection and workflow.** Reconstruct a relevant past outcome and available weekly delivery capacity. Immediately produce a user-reviewed, opportunity-specific sample or proposal outline. Connect service scope, time budget, milestones and contingency capacity; rehearse a real client discussion, then refine the proposal. If hired, convert exactly what was sold into a realistic delivery sequence and a reusable case study with client permission.

**Incremental value.** Positioning and sales promises affect delivery feasibility. A polished pitch disconnected from actual time/capacity can create a bad first engagement. The compound job ends with a delivered paid project, not a CV or reminders.

**Payment/native/AI.** A limited return-to-practice program might command payment after showing access to a real opportunity. AI can draft and challenge scope with evidence from the user's work. Native is weak: voice capture and calendar access help, but desktop work is central. Human feedback on commercial judgment is likely valuable.

**Expertise and strongest failure.** No earnings guarantee, invented credentials, legal contract advice or sending pitches without approval. A human network or career coach may be the true product; general AI and free templates cover much of the software. The weak native rationale is a substantive reason to deprioritize for this objective.

## C7. Host a meal where everyone can participate and I can enjoy it

**Customer and trigger.** A woman who normally organizes extended-family meals, hosting a specific holiday/milestone in four weeks for guests with differing confirmed food and accessibility needs, a fixed budget and a constrained kitchen. Gender describes the recruitment cohort; it is not evidence that only women perform or need this job.

**Likely language.** “I want everyone to be fed comfortably, and I want to sit down with them instead of spending the day solving problems.”

**Current combination to investigate.** Recipe sites, guest messages, grocery ordering, spreadsheets, saved menus, takeout/catering and help from relatives.

**Connection and workflow.** Confirm guest constraints and kitchen capacity. Immediately propose a feasible menu with one make-ahead component and a budget. Resolve shopping quantities, oven/stovetop contention, delivery lead times and voluntarily accepted helper jobs together. Rehearse an unfamiliar dish or choose purchased alternatives. Provide an executable cooking sequence; save only the elements that worked for the next event.

**Incremental value.** A set of suitable recipes can still fail because of equipment timing, cross-contact or missing helpers. Menu, quantity, equipment and serving sequence are practical dependencies. The outcome is a completed shared occasion.

**Payment/native/AI.** A per-event plan could be compared with meal planning or catering spend; neither proves app demand. Native helps at the grocery store and in the kitchen. AI can organize constrained menus and sequence reviewed recipes, while people confirm allergens and ingredients.

**Expertise and strongest failure.** Food safety and severe allergies need authoritative guidance and explicit human verification; software cannot guarantee an allergen-free meal. Free recipes, general AI and a caterer may cover the entire outcome. Holiday urgency is relevant to Q4 but seasonality and no established paid gap keep this below the three researched candidates.

## The test all seven must pass

For the same customer, show (1) an existing combination used on a real occasion, (2) a consequential failure *between* parts, (3) a connected result that beats the strongest part plus ordinary tools, and (4) payment that covers delivering that extra result. If only one part earns money, revise to that proposition or reject the bundle. Adding adjacent prevalence estimates or more features cannot supply this evidence.
