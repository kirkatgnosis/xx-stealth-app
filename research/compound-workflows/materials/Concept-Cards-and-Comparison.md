# Competing concept cards and outcome comparisons

Research stimuli, not claims about a product that exists. Read after behavior interviews. Rotate card order; omit the concept IDs and researcher notes in participant-facing copies. Use the same visual treatment for all options. Prices here are hypotheses for a limited digital program; the later human concierge has a separately disclosed price and scope.

## Work return: participant cards

**Option A — A practical work capsule. $99 for one preparation package.** “Using the clothes that fit and feel comfortable now, we help you put together outfits for your first work situations. You receive combinations from your own wardrobe, explanations, and a short list of any useful purchases or alterations within your budget. A stylist reviews the result. You choose what to wear and what, if anything, to buy.”

**Option B — Get ready for the actual workdays. $149 for four weeks.** “Start with the same reviewed work capsule. Then try it against your actual commute, work situations and clothing-care routine. A practical rehearsal helps reveal what does not work before the first day. During your first weeks, the plan changes using what you actually wore and what needs adjusting. Purchases and alterations stay within the budget you choose.”

**Option C — Keep my current approach.** “Use my existing clothes, advice, apps, notes and shopping process. I do not buy a preparation package.”

**Plain-language storyboard:** A real workday → relevant items already owned → a usable first outfit → a rehearsal in real conditions → one justified change → an outfit actually worn → next-day reuse. Ask participants to point to the step that changes an action. If only the outfit selection matters, record that against the bundle.

## Active trip: participant cards

**Option A — Prepare for my itinerary. $99 for an eight-week digital program.** “A qualified exercise professional reviews a preparation program around your operator's stated walking demands, available time and current routine. You get an agreed sequence of sessions and reviewed alternatives for ordinary scheduling changes. It does not provide medical clearance or certify that a trip is safe.”

**Option B — Prepare with the equipment I will actually take. $199 for eight weeks.** “Start with the same reviewed preparation program. Link practice outings to trying your intended footwear, layers and pack. Use what happens to make practical equipment and preparation decisions before departure, and finish with a packing plan based on what you tested. Your operator remains the source for trip requirements and local conditions.”

**Option C — Keep my current approach.** “Use the operator's included guidance, my current workouts or coach, gear advice and any existing apps. I do not buy another preparation program.”

**Storyboard:** Actual itinerary → agreed first preparation week → a real outing with intended equipment → a specific observation → a reviewed practical adjustment → a repeat trial → tested packing choices. The link between the outing and a useful changed decision is the hypothesis.

These cards do not establish that the $100 trip premium is accepted. HikeStrong's $19.99 monthly/$149.99 annual offer is a particularly important current reference, and its itinerary-specific features must be disclosed in the comparison. [HikeStrong US listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/hikestrong-train-for-hiking/id6771225440).

## Reserve card: midlife capacity

“Keep the strength program and food routines you already value. When the week changes, work out a feasible combination of sessions, familiar meals and rest around an activity you want to preserve. You approve the tradeoffs.” Compare with the best existing strength service and its adjacent tools, then a simple reusable planning/AI template. No launch price is recommended; the independent research track's $15–20 monthly incremental offer is only a possible later falsification test. This reserve does not enter the initial paid test.

## Researcher protocol

1. Ask the participant to explain each option back in her own words, including what is not supplied. Correct misunderstanding before recording preference.
2. Ask which she would choose *today*, including none; ask what she believes the extra payment buys. Do not interpret a card choice as a purchase.
3. Give the same real inputs to the current approach and an equally competent component baseline. For work, allow ordinary stylist intake to ask about commute/comfort; for travel, allow a good trainer to ask about pack and terrain. Artificially weakening the component invalidates the test.
4. Compare the connection with the baseline on a specific actual occasion. Counterbalance order across eight people; use different comparable occasions to reduce learning carryover. With small samples, describe sequence effects rather than claim experimental certainty.
5. Hold core content and planned expert contact time constant in this comparison. For any consequential safety issue, share it immediately rather than withholding it to create an experimental contrast.
6. Record changed decisions, customer effort, expert minutes, corrections and completed real tasks. A prettier card, more output or greater confidence alone is not a pass.

**Work example:** both approaches receive the same 15 items, three work situations, comfort requirements and budget. Both can recommend a capsule. The connected approach adds a timed rehearsal and actual wear feedback. Determine whether it finds a *new* consequential issue and whether the correction is worn successfully. A one-time intake improvement may be enough; if so, record that instead of claiming ongoing software value.

**Trip example:** both approaches receive the same confirmed itinerary, expert-reviewed training and equipment currently owned. Both can discuss gear. The connected approach explicitly pairs a practice outing with a gear trial and reviewed decision. Determine whether a useful change occurs beyond what the trainer/operator already advised. The participant performs only activities appropriate to the actual reviewed program.

**General AI baseline prompt:** “Help me organize this upcoming situation using only the confirmed details below. Separate missing information from facts. Give me a short sequence of decisions and questions, using what I already have and my stated budget. Do not invent fit, availability, clinical conclusions or trip-safety assurances. Here are my current resources and constraints: [participant-approved redacted information].” Use only with explicit artifact-processing consent. Its quality is measured, not assumed.

After comparisons, ask: “Which step would you remove? What would you pay someone to handle? What still requires you or another person to do the work? Would the simpler option be enough?” Do not ask participants to validate our rationale.
