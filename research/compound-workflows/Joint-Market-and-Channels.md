**Definitions:** C2's event is return after at least six months away/predominantly remote; its joint filter requires an actual client-facing/hybrid setting plus a current wardrobe/day mismatch and desire to act. C3's event is a booked guided active holiday with preparation runway; its joint filter includes ordinary walking scope and an unresolved interaction between preparation and equipment. C1's event is a current body/life disruption affecting a desired activity; its joint filter includes prior regular/paid strength and interacting weekly constraints.

Serviceability combines English, compatible initial phone platform, ability to use the workflow, geography/provider coverage and appropriate scope.45–65% is an assumption, not a Pew iPhone rate. Smartphone ownership must not be multiplied in again. These factors are conditional scenarios rather than a claim that marginal concerns are statistically independent.

The event ranges have **no retrieved empirical basis for these exact definitions**. They are a sensitivity scaffold, not newly discovered prevalence. We do not call them a dollar TAM or multiply by a guessed willingness-to-pay rate. A national range this wide cannot select a product. C2 care-return and remote-return must be measured separately; do not add them without deduplicating people. A caregiver can also travel and train: no totals across concepts are meaningful.

## How to replace the unknowns

Interviews reveal definitions and behavior; they do not measure national overlap. For a population estimate, use a probability-based or appropriately weighted US survey with the whole joint screen, a specified 12-month event window, and enough *qualified* respondents to estimate the scarce intersection. Report recruitment yield, weighting, nonresponse and uncertainty. Exact timing matters: a birth count, labor-force reentrant stock or active-travel participation count does not measure the imminent buying occasion.

Before paying for that survey, obtain more decision-useful **channel-conditioned counts**. An authorized returnship/stylist partner can count unique confirmed returns fitting the screen. A small operator can count unique US booked travelers in relevant age/itinerary/departure windows. Obtain aggregate counts without customer contact details initially, then permissioned introductions if authorized. These measure a serviceable audience within that source, not national prevalence. Count the joint criteria directly rather than multiplying unrelated audience percentages.

For willingness to pay, record actual displayed offer, denominator, service selected, money charged, refunds and whether the person was already compensated for research. Paid styling or travel is background; payment for the actual connecting service is the relevant signal. The same people cannot be assumed to buy at $99,$149 and $199.

Gross buyers are counted before refunds; the unit model separately provisions refunds. The duplication allowance is a sensitivity, not known overlap. In actual measurement use unique attributed customers, agreement-specific commissions and real cost records. These illustrative channels produce CAC above the model's $30/$40 assumptions, and far fewer purchases than its 494/924 fully settled founder-compensated hurdles. Neither implies that higher traffic retains the same conversion or cost.

The zero-access case is **zero sales**, not 27 or 38. A named operator or large newsletter is not a secured route. Current incumbents have existing relationships; new suppliers must earn permission and trust. No ad CPC/CAC, SEO ranking, influencer response or app-store conversion was measured. Paid ads are not added as a residual number to make the target work.

The financial base separately models 0/40/80 native purchases in October/November/December. That is an explicit sales requirement scenario; the channel examples above do **not** support those volumes. Under its two-month settlement approximation, no November/December proceeds arrive by December 31. Actual fiscal payment dates supersede that approximation.

## Could either be appropriately sized beyond the deadline?

A modest sustainable business is conceivable in arithmetic. At the digital-pass base contributions, **2,000 annual C2 purchases** yield $298,000 gross and $81,036 contribution after acquisition/delivery; after $12,000 annual fixed operations and $36,000 founder compensation, about $33,036 remains before startup recovery and taxes. **2,000 C3 purchases** produce $398,000 gross but only about $1,236 after the analogous $18,000 fixed/$36,000 draw;4,000 purchases would leave about $56,472. These are episode purchases, not subscription ARR, and assume no unmodeled step-up in fixed costs.

Those volumes are not established obtainable markets. At 4% offer conversion they require 50,000 or 100,000 *qualified offer views* per year, plus replacement for refunds as modeled. Human capacity is material:2,000 C2 cases require about 1,500 expert/support hours at the assumed limits;4,000 C3 cases require about 5,000. Those contractor costs are in variable delivery, but recruitment, quality control and management can add fixed cost. The business question is whether a repeatable channel and delivery system can support that volume, not whether adjacent national TAMs sum to billions.
