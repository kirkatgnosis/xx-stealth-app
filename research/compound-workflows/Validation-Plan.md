# Validation before an MVP specification

Prepared September 12, 2026. **Everything below is proposed fieldwork.** No recruitment, contact, interviews, ads, deposits, purchases or payments have been conducted. The desk work, source checks, models and materials are completed research. Dates assume prompt authorization and access; move them rather than silently compress observation windows.

The first question is whether the connection earns its cost. The second is whether a native consumer business can deliver it profitably. A successful human service answers neither question automatically.

The stages can overlap for recruitment and review, but outcomes cannot be observed before they happen. The C2 four-week paid service starts roughly two weeks before a confirmed return, so it includes actual workday use. Fresh paid recruits need a return in 10–18 days at service start; the broader 4–8 week context-interview screen is not the delivery-start rule. A C2 service starting October 19 and returning around November 2 ends around November 16. An eight-week C3 service ends around December 14; a later trip is followed up only when it occurs. Building and settling a new native app after that will generally miss the December cash deadline in the conservative model. An earlier authorized start or existing channel may change the timeline. Do not solve this conflict by relabeling concierge sales as native-app proof.

## Why these thresholds

The context sample is intentionally small and mixed, so counts are used to decide whether to spend on the next step. Six independent concrete episodes reduces dependence on a memorable anecdote; it does not estimate market size. A majority showing incremental value is necessary because everyone would bear the bundle's setup and delivery cost. Five full-price purchases is a practical minimum to examine repeat delivery rather than one unusually enthusiastic customer.

The money thresholds come from the commercial model. At the test service prices, W $199 and T $299, an 8% refund provision and domestic card fees leave about $177.01 and $266.11 before delivery. With assumed delivery of $113.50/$188 and acquisition of $30/$45, contribution is only about $33.51/$33.11. A single extra hour of expert work can erase it. The program should fail the commercial gate if its human workload consistently exceeds that budget, even when participants like it.

For the eventual digital passes, W $149 and T $199, the modeled 15% store fee and 8% refunds leave $116.52/$155.62. The assumed full delivery budgets are $46/$88 and CAC $30/$40. Those budgets require expert review to fall to 30/45 minutes and ordinary support to 15/30 minutes per episode. **Those reductions must be demonstrated, not obtained by deleting labor from a spreadsheet.** Use ten completed examples, a consistent review rubric and a second qualified reviewer for a small blinded sample. Expose all exceptions. Recompute costs if quality requires more work.

Five to ten customers cannot establish a low refund rate. Zero refunds among ten still leaves substantial uncertainty; the rough “rule of three” gives an upper risk near 30%, under simplifying independent-trial assumptions. Retain the 8% planning provision and test 12% or higher until larger mature cohorts justify replacing it. Likewise, the 95% Wilson interval for 5/20 purchases is approximately 11%–47%; a recruited, interested group is not a random acquisition sample, so even that interval understates transport uncertainty.

## Recruitment and budget

Use the [recruitment/interview guide](materials/Recruitment-and-Interview-Guide.md). Recruit C2 care-return and remote-return separately. For C3, book date, departure date and operator demands matter more than aspirational interest in travel. Include satisfied incumbent users and those who declined or abandoned paid alternatives. Do not recruit only fashion enthusiasts, fitness devotees, personal friends or an expert's superfans.

Start with moderator-approved invitations, relevant independent practitioners, and one small operator/return-to-work organization willing to introduce eligible people. These are potential routes, not secured access. No private directories, unsolicited direct messages or employee/client lists have been accessed. [iRelaunch](https://www.irelaunch.com/), [Trailblazer's operator model](https://trailblazerwellness.com/tour-operators/).

If permissioned recruitment fails, a paid panel is a fallback requiring authorization. Respondent advertises moderated pay-as-you-go recruitment at $40 B2 C/$80 B2 B per completed session, incentives extra; a professional-return screen may be classified B2 B. For 24 interviews, recruitment alone is $960–$1,920; assumed $40 incentives add $960. Budget another assumed $600–$1,200 for expert comparison preparation and $400 for administration/tools: **$2,920–$4,480** before paid-pilot labor. Founder interviewing/analysis/recruiting could take 25–40 hours, separately costed at an assumed $30/hour ($750–$1,200). These are ceilings to review, not purchases made or vendor quotes for the exact sample. [Respondent pricing](https://www.respondent.io/pricing).

An interview incentive must never finance a later payment that is counted as demand. Use fresh paid-offer prospects where feasible; report compensated research participants separately. Pilot delivery at ten bundle customers per concept costs up to $1,135/$1,880 under the stated labor assumptions, before acquisition and fixed research work. Sales may offset some costs; do not prespend uncollected receipts. This validation spend is part of the startup envelope, not an extra cost silently omitted from break-even.

## Connection and native-app tests

Use the [concept cards](materials/Concept-Cards-and-Comparison.md) with equally credible presentation, the same customer inputs, the same core expert content and matched expert attention. Always include the strongest available component and “keep my current approach.” General AI plus the customer's ordinary tools is another real baseline, with its limitations explained. A free incumbent trial may be inspected only after explicit authorization to enroll if it creates an account or future charge.

Do not make app opens a success measure. For C2, count appropriate outfits actually worn on relevant days, corrections after rehearsal and useful re-use. For C3, count agreed preparation sessions, completed gear trials, corrected packing/preparation decisions, and post-trip usefulness when the trip occurs. No physiological improvement or prevention claim is established by these small tests.

Before specifying native software, have paid digital-program customers complete repeated tasks through their existing phone workflow. Record camera/receipt/voice/offline needs and failures that a native capability could resolve. Evidence of recurring context-specific inconvenience supports a native implementation hypothesis; satisfaction with a PDF does not. Do not force an installation to manufacture dependence on native features.

## Payment, renewal and repetition

Use [the paid-test protocol](materials/Concierge-Protocol.md). A $20 refundable reservation, if later chosen, establishes less commitment than a full-price service purchase; report it separately with expiry, refund and delivery terms. Avoid deposits when an immediately deliverable small service is feasible. Do not take payment for nonexistent software. TestFlight payments are simulated and do not count.

C2: observe whether a buyer pays for a later *real* season, role or event, after the return is finished. C3: observe whether a buyer pays for preparation of another *booked* trip. Neither can be inferred from “would use again,” a saved card, app entitlement or operator rebooking. Repeat purchase is zero in the base model until observed. Annual renewal cannot be observed by December for a new Q4 subscription.

## Gate before writing an MVP specification

One candidate must have a specific customer/trigger, repeatedly observed interaction problem, a component comparison that changes actual outcomes, independent paid commitment, credible reduced-service delivery, a reachable paid-acquisition path and an honest updated cash case. State the remaining uncertainties in the specification brief; do not promote them to requirements as though solved.

If only concierge demand survives, the conclusion is a service opportunity. If only styling or training survives, the conclusion is a component opportunity. If neither survives at an economic price, reject both. A new compound hypothesis may then deserve research, but another round of desk ranking should not substitute for the missing customer signal.
