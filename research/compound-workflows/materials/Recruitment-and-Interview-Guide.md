# Recruitment and behavior interview materials

Drafts for an authorized future study. Nothing has been posted or sent. Recruit adults only; US-first, English-capable for this study, any phone platform during context interviews. Record iPhone/Android for eventual serviceability rather than confusing device choice with need.

## Recruitment profiles

**C2 required:** actual return to client-facing or hybrid/in-person work in 4–8 weeks; at least six months mainly away from that setting through care leave or remote work; a present practical clothing/day mismatch; willing to discuss one real upcoming work situation. Do not require shopping enthusiasm or willingness to buy an app. Record care-return and remote-return as separate segments. A birth is neither necessary nor sufficient. The national sizing envelope uses ages 30–59 for comparison; age outside this range can be recorded as an exploratory interview and must not be silently added to its denominator.

**C3 required:** paid booking/deposit for a guided walking/active holiday departing 8–12 weeks from enrollment; an ordinary nontechnical itinerary; can describe current preparation and one open decision. Initial sizing envelope 45–64, with adjacent ages recorded separately. Include people satisfied with operator guidance. Exclude requests for rehabilitation, diagnosis or medical clearance from this service study; an expert can determine whether proposed exercise work is within scope.

For each set of 12, aim for four who are content with current alternatives, four who spent money but abandoned/changed a solution, and four with unresolved friction. These are recruitment quotas, not assumed population shares. If they cannot be filled, record actual composition and do not invent a balanced sample. Seek different budgets, bodies, access needs and experience levels; do not use weight-loss intent as a screen.

## Screener, seven questions

1. Which specific work return or active trip is already planned, and on what date? What has actually been confirmed? A redacted calendar/booking is optional; a narrative answer can qualify, with verification status recorded.
2. For work: how long since you routinely worked in this setting, and what setting will you return to? For travel: operator, ordinary activity level and preparation period?
3. Tell us about the last practical preparation decision you made for it. What did you do?
4. Which products or people have you used for it in the past six months? Which did you pay for, if any? “None” is a valid answer.
5. Is there anything about your current approach you would keep exactly as it is?
6. What phone do you use, and are there practical access requirements for a short conversation or optional artifact exercise?
7. Are you comfortable talking about this voluntarily for 30–40 minutes? Recording and photos are optional and separately consented.

Do not screen with “Would an AI app that connects everything help?” Do not reveal the preferred bundle before the interview. Store screener nonqualifiers and reason codes separately from participants so recruitment yield can be calculated without retaining unnecessary personal information.

## Draft invitation

**Work return:** “We are researching how people prepare for a return to client-facing or office work after a period at home or working remotely. We would like to hear about the clothes, practical decisions and tools you actually use. This is a 30–40 minute research conversation, not a sales call. We are interested in approaches that work well as well as frustrations. Optional redacted examples are welcome; no body photos are required. Participation and any recording are voluntary. The study offers a $40 thank-you if this research budget is approved.”

**Active trip:** “We are researching how women prepare for a guided walking or active holiday they have already booked. We would like to understand how you handle preparation, clothing and equipment decisions, including what your operator already provides. This is a 30–40 minute research conversation, not a training or sales session. Optional redacted examples are welcome. Participation and any recording are voluntary. The study offers a $40 thank-you if this research budget is approved.”

Before any authorized sending, replace the last sentence with the actual approved amount or remove it; fill the study contact, dates, storage/deletion policy and scheduling route. Moderator/operator introductions are subject to their permission. No outreach consent is implied by this draft.

## Opening script and handling of artifacts

“We have not selected a product. We want to understand what happened in a real situation, including things that worked. You can skip any question or stop. We do not need medical records, private client names, identifiable booking numbers or financial account information. You can describe purchases rather than sharing receipts. May we take notes? Recording is separate and optional.”

Use a study ID; keep contact details in a separate access-controlled list. Prefer redacted examples and private optional clothed photos. Let participants choose words about fit, comfort and appearance. Do not infer body size, health status, income or household role from images. Propose deleting raw recordings/photos 30 days after analysis, subject to an actual agreed research notice before collection. Do not upload artifacts to an AI service without specific informed permission describing that use.

## Behavior interview,35 minutes

**0–5 minutes: the occasion.** “Walk me through the day or trip you are preparing for. What would make it go well? What are you most looking forward to? What is already settled?”

**5–15 minutes: reconstruct the last actual episode.** “Start at the last moment you decided something needed doing. What did you open or who did you ask first? What happened next? What did you buy, change, return, book or abandon? Show the sequence if convenient. Where did one decision force you to revisit another?” Probe dates and concrete actions; do not rename ordinary inconvenience as a problem.

**15–22 minutes: payments and substitutions.** “What have you spent on this preparation, separate from the clothes/trip itself? What did each payment include? Did you buy again, renew, cancel or ask for money back? What did you try free first? What was worth the price? What looked worth paying for but wasn't? If you could keep only one of these services, which would you keep, and why?” Record approximate amounts as self-report and receipts as optional artifacts, not verified revenue.

**22–28 minutes: interaction and effort.** “Tell me about a time information or advice from one place did not fit another part of the day. What happened as a result? Who resolved it? How long did that take? When did your current approach work without extra effort? Was more information, a decision, practice, another person's help or actual labor missing?”

**28–33 minutes: completed outcomes and repetition.** “What have you actually used or finished? What did you stop doing? When did you need help again? What would happen if you used your present approach for the coming occasion?”

**33–35 minutes: close.** “What should I have asked? Is there a part of preparing for this that people misunderstand?” Ask separate permission for a later research invitation if useful. Do not present concepts until the behavior record is complete.

## Candidate probes, used only when relevant

**C2:** last item bought/returned/altered; an outfit that looked right but did not work through the day; commute, temperature, seated comfort, laundry or access constraint; who knows the workplace norms; what ordinary stylist intake already covers; actual item count needed. Ask “What feels like you?” without suggesting a style.

**C3:** what came with the booking; an actual recent preparation walk; a gear trial and decision it changed; who interpreted distance/elevation demands; schedule interruptions; what she will ask the guide; whether she even wants to use a phone during the trip. Do not assess fitness or prescribe exercise in an interview.

## Analysis record

For each participant record: qualification and verification; current stack; dated payment/self-report; sequence of actions; concrete interaction failure or no failure; consequence; workaround; current satisfaction; avoided/abandoned spend; human dependency; important quote with consent; contradiction to the hypothesis. Mark quoted language separately from researcher inference. Code the episode before viewing concept preference.

At the end, produce an evidence matrix with counts and counterexamples. Keep nonbuyers, satisfied users and failed recruitment visible. Do not report these purposive proportions as market prevalence or statistical proof.
